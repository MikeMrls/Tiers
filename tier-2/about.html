About

Between the ticking and the shelving

Adrian W. Mercer is a novelist, essayist, and cultural historian. His books are about what places remember and what people carry.

Adrian William Mercer was born on September 14, 1978, in a terraced house in the north of England, the second of three children. His father repaired clocks; his mother ran the lending desk at the local library. He grew up, he likes to say, between the ticking and the shelving, and he has been interested in time and in stories ever since.

He studied history, then architectural conservation, and spent most of his twenties surveying old buildings: measuring rooms nobody lived in anymore, photographing staircases before they were removed, writing reports on houses that were about to become something else. He started writing in the margins of those reports. The notes became essays, and the essays, eventually, became a novel.

The story so far

The Architecture of Silence (2012) grew out of a real afternoon in a condemned farmhouse, where Mercer found a drawer of letters that had never been sent. The novel imagines who might have written them and why they stayed in the dark. It was widely praised as a debut of unusual patience.

Maps of a Vanishing City (2016) followed five years of walking the same streets with a notebook, recording the bookshops, cinemas, and bathhouses that closed along the way. The Weight of Ordinary Things (2020) turned the same attention inward, to the objects and rituals of a single life.

Mercer’s writing has a contemplative, literary style that blends historical detail with a contemporary eye. He writes regularly on architecture, memory, and culture; recent essays include “The Stories Hidden Inside Old Buildings” and “The Quiet Disappearance of Independent Bookshops.”

He is at work on a new novel, and he is happiest in old buildings, second-hand bookshops, and other people’s attics.
