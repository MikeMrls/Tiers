No. 01 · 2020

The Weight of Ordinary Things

Open the book →
Mercer can hold a teacup up to the light and show you three generations of a family. These essays are small in subject and enormous in feeling.

The Northgate Review

Essays to be read slowly, then read again.

Hollis Literary Quarterly

Quietly funny, rigorously observed, and more moving than a book about drawers has any right to be.

Meridian Books Weekly

I bought this for my mother and ended up keeping it. Every chapter made me look around my own kitchen differently.

Ellen M.
★★★★★ Reader review

No. 02 · 2016

Maps of a Vanishing City

Open the book →
A field guide to everything a city forgets. You will never walk past a boarded-up shopfront the same way again.

The City Desk

Part history, part elegy, wholly absorbing.

The Northgate Review

It sent me looking for the street where my grandparents’ bakery used to be. It’s a parking lot now. I stood there for an hour.

Daniel R.
★★★★★ Reader review

No. 03 · 2012

The Architecture of Silence

Open the book →
Mercer writes about houses the way other novelists write about people: with tenderness, suspicion, and a long memory.

The Lantern Literary Supplement

A debut of rare patience. The letters under the stairs will stay with you long after the last page.

Meridian Books Weekly

Slow in the best way. I read the last fifty pages in one sitting and then sat in silence for a while.
