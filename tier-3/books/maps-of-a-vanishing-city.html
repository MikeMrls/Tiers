Maps of a Vanishing City

Fourteen essays on the streets we lose, and what they take with them.

Buy on Amazon ↗ All retailers

Synopsis

Over five years Adrian W. Mercer walked the same city again and again, notebook in hand, recording what disappeared: a bookshop, a cinema, a tram depot, the public baths where three generations learned to swim.

Maps of a Vanishing City collects fourteen essays on urban change and the quiet ways neighborhoods fade, and on what happens to our sense of self when the places that made us are gone.

Part history, part elegy, it is a field guide to the city we carry in our heads.

Excerpt · From the essay “The Last Day of Halloway’s”

On the morning Halloway’s closed, the bell over the door rang two hundred and six times. I know because Margaret Halloway counted, standing behind the till where her father had stood, and his father before him, making a pencil mark on the back of an envelope each time someone came in. Most of them did not buy anything. They had come, they said, to have a last look.

A city does not vanish all at once. It goes one shop at a time, one bus route, one bench, one public clock that stops at twenty past three and is never wound again. You barely notice. Then one day you give a stranger directions, left at the bookshop, right at the baths, and realize that you are describing a place that no longer exists. A map that only you can read.
