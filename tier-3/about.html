Known for reflective prose and carefully observed characters, Mercer writes where literary fiction meets cultural criticism and philosophy.

His work examines how personal histories shape collective memory, how cities keep the stories of the people who live in them, and how ordinary moments reveal extraordinary truths. He blends historical detail with a contemporary eye.

The story so far

Adrian William Mercer was born on September 14, 1978, in a terraced house in the north of England, the second of three children. His father repaired clocks; his mother ran the lending desk at the local library. He grew up, he likes to say, between the ticking and the shelving.

He studied history, then architectural conservation, and spent most of his twenties surveying old buildings: measuring rooms nobody lived in anymore, photographing staircases before they were removed, writing reports on houses that were about to become something else.

the survey reports are still in a box in his attic

He started writing in the margins of those reports. The notes became essays, and the essays, eventually, became a novel. The Architecture of Silence (2012) grew out of an afternoon in a condemned farmhouse where he found a drawer of letters that had never been sent.

Maps of a Vanishing City (2016) followed five years of walking the same streets with a notebook. The Weight of Ordinary Things (2020) turned the same attention inward, to the objects and rituals of a single life.

new novel in progress. he won’t say what it’s about. (a lighthouse?)

He writes regularly on architecture, memory, and culture, and he is happiest in old buildings, second-hand bookshops, and other people’s attics.
